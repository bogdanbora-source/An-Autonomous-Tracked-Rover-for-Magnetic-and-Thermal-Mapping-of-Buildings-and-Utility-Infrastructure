# An-Autonomous-Tracked-Rover-for-Magnetic-and-Thermal-Mapping-of-Buildings-and-Utility-Infrastructure
Presenting the design, implementation, and calibration of a low-cost autonomous tracked rover that surveys an area to map the static magnetic field and surface infrared temperature. The platform combines a tracked chassis, an arduino 32-bit microcontroller, a three-axis magnetometer, a thermal sensor, a gps module, ultrasonic sensor.

The platform combines a tracked chassis, an arduino 32-bit microcontroller, a three-axis magnetometer, a thermal sensor, a gps module, ultrasonic sensor, and on-board logging. The magnetic sensor detects ferromagnetic and electrically active infrastructure steel water and gas pipes, reinforcing bars, electrical conduit, transformers, and cables which perturb the local geomagnetic field. The thermal sensor detects the surface temperature signatures of concealed heat sources, heat loss, and fluid leaks. The central design principle is correlation because a leaking pipe presents both a magnetic signature, from the metal of the pipe, and a thermal signature, from the temperature of the escaping fluid, the coincidence of the two anomalies at a single coordinate is a stronger diagnostic than either parameter alone. The behaviour of the rover is built around a stop and sample logging strategy that eliminates motor-induced magnetic noise and registers the two sensors in space and time, a calibration methodology progressing from a library routine to a full ellipsoid fit and a post-processing pipeline that renders per-modality heatmaps, a correlated dual-modality overlay, an interactive georeferenced map, and a stacked three-dimensional visualisation for multi-storey surveys. The intended applications are domestic and small-institutional in scale: locating buried utilities, tracing structural steel, and detecting pipe leaks and heat loss in houses and apartment blocks.
Keywords: magnetometer survey; infrared thermography; autonomous robotics; utility location; leak detection;
